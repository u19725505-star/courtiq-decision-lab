01 / The research questions

Put the basketball question on the scoreboard.

These are the questions the experiment is designed to investigate. They are deliberately phrased as tests—not promises of winning games.

01
Primary question
Q1

Can shot context and defensive pressure improve prediction of a make on a held-out game?

Compare a shot-context baseline with variants that add contest level, closest-defender distance, and engineered interactions.

Q2

Which defensive signal adds the clearest predictive value?

Evaluate discrimination and probability quality together; do not choose a model using a single metric alone.

Q3

Does the improvement persist across games—or depend on a few outliers?

Inspect game-level performance and paired game-cluster bootstrap intervals.

Q4

What spatial questions can we responsibly answer with the fields currently available?

Explore a rotatable court schematic and distance concepts while keeping the missing real shot coordinates explicit.

The hypothesis

Context should beat a context-free guess.

The test is whether added defensive context improves held-out predictions beyond the baseline, with uncertainty reported.

How to read this

ROC-AUC measures ranking discrimination. Average precision summarizes precision across recall levels. Brier score measures squared probability error. Log loss penalizes confident wrong predictions.

The contest-only variant leads ROC-AUC and Brier score. The variant with both defensive features has the highest reported average precision and slightly better log loss. There is no single winner across every metric.

What limits the confidence?

Only ten game clusters contribute to the game-level robustness analysis. Intervals can be unstable at this scale. The next serious test is validation on more games and an independent dataset, ideally with a pre-registered evaluation protocol.
