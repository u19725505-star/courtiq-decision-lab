01 / The question

Great basketball is more than a make or a miss.

A result tells us what happened. Decision intelligence asks what context surrounded the attempt—and whether the evidence helps distinguish shot outcomes beyond a simple baseline.

01
The research question

Can shot context and defensive-pressure signals help estimate the probability of a make on a previously unseen game?

Why it matters

Context over box-score hindsight

Distance, shot clock, shot type, contest level and defender proximity can add context to the outcome.

What we test

Predictive evidence

Compare saved out-of-fold probabilities with the same held-out shots and evaluate discrimination and probability quality.

What we do not claim

No causal shortcut

A predictive association does not prove that a defensive variable caused a miss or that a strategy will win games.

The guiding principle: make the model earn its place. Compare against a baseline, inspect uncertainty, and keep the limits visible.

03 / The method

Hold out the game. Test the evidence.

The evaluation uses saved out-of-fold predictions from game-held-out validation. The point is not to memorize individual shots from a game and then congratulate the model for recognizing them.

03
STEP 01

Prepare

Validate the binary target, stable shot IDs and usable feature fields.

STEP 02

Hold out a game

Use game-grouped evaluation so each held-out game's outcomes are predicted out of fold.

STEP 03

Compare variants

Evaluate five saved probability streams using common classification and probabilistic metrics.

STEP 04

Audit robustness

Inspect per-game performance, calibration and uncertainty—not just one headline score.

What each metric asks

ROC-AUC — can the model rank makes above misses?

Average precision — how useful is precision across recall levels?

Brier score — how close are predicted probabilities to outcomes?

Log loss — how severely are confident wrong probabilities penalized?

Why use more than one?

A model can rank outcomes better while producing imperfect probabilities. The evidence must be read as a set: discrimination, calibration, error, and variation across games.

OOF means out-of-fold. It is stronger evidence than in-sample fit, but it is not a substitute for an independent external dataset.

05 / Shot intelligence

From a single probability to a decision lens.

A useful model does not label a shot “good” in isolation. It estimates an outcome probability under the observed feature context; a coaching interpretation needs basketball context, quality data and validation.

05

Visual explainer only. The heat rings illustrate the idea of distance zones; the marks are not reconstructed shot events or a measured court heatmap.

Decision lens

Probability is not destiny.

A predicted 0.60 is an estimate, not a promise that a particular shot goes in.

Context changes the question.

Distance, contest and time pressure can help explain why superficially similar attempts may have different predicted outcomes.

What this prototype can support

Research exploration, feature comparison, probability auditing and game-held-out evaluation.

What it cannot yet support

Precise shot-location recommendations, causal defensive prescriptions or claims of live game-winning impact.

06 / Robustness

Averages are the beginning, not the finish line.

With only 10 games, performance can vary with the game mix. The research audit therefore inspects game-level scores and uses game-cluster uncertainty analysis with an explicit small-cluster warning.

07 / Limitations & next plays

Trust is part of the design.

The strongest competition story is not that every question has been solved. It is that the project can show its evidence, identify its weak points and define the next experiment.

07
Known limits

What remains unresolved

  • Only ten games are available for the current evaluation.
  • No genuine court x/y coordinates in the current engineered table.
  • Model performance is moderate, not near-perfect.
  • Predictive associations do not establish causation.
  • Independent external-game validation remains necessary.
Next plays

Where to take CourtIQ

  • Recover authentic source shot coordinates and document units.
  • Expand game coverage and test on held-out seasons or teams.
  • Report uncertainty and calibration by distance and contest segments.
  • Evaluate strategy changes prospectively before claiming impact.
  • Make every chart traceable to saved tables and code.

A decision tool earns trust not by sounding certain, but by showing what it knows, what it doesn't, and how it was tested.

The final play

Make the next decision a better-informed one.

CourtIQ is an evidence-driven basketball decision lab: shot context, defensive-pressure features, out-of-fold evaluation and game-level robustness brought into one transparent story. The current results support further investigation—not a claim that the work is finished.
